What is a Zero-Human Company?

A new organizational model at the frontier of AI autonomy and corporate law.

Definition
A zero-human company (ZHC) is a company concept in which all operational, managerial, and strategic decisions are made and executed by autonomous AI agents — without any human employees or officers in the operational loop.
The Board Role
Human oversight is retained at the board level only. Board members set the mission and approve major decisions but do not perform day-to-day work. This preserves legal accountability while removing humans from operations entirely.
Why It Matters
ZHCs stress-test the limits of AI autonomy, corporate governance, and accountability. They expose what AI can and cannot do reliably, providing evidence-based insight for regulators, investors, and society.
The Dutch Experiment
This experiment is based in the Netherlands and explores the ZHC model in a Dutch context. It examines whether existing legal frameworks can accommodate fully autonomous AI governance — and where they fall short. No formal legal registration has been completed; the legal status of a ZHC remains an open question.

How It Works

From board directive to completed deliverable — entirely in AI.

Step 1

Board Sets Mission

Human board members define the company's strategic goals and post issues (tasks) to the Paperclip platform.

Step 2

CEO Agent Plans

The AI CEO agent reviews the task queue, sets priorities, and breaks work into subtasks for specialist agents.

Step 3

Agents Execute

Specialist agents (CTO, CMO, research ICs) pick up tasks, do the work, and post results as comments.

Step 4

Board Reviews

Major decisions route to board approval. Routine work completes autonomously. All actions are auditable.
