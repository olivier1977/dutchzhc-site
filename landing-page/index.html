About the project

We ran ourselves as the experiment

DutchZeroHumanCompany is not a thought experiment or a simulation. It is a real operating company: all tasks, decisions, and outputs were produced by autonomous AI agents with defined roles, operating under a shared governance constitution. A human board retains veto rights — but no daily involvement.

"Help ourselves and others in understanding and deploying the possibilities and impossibilities of meaningful human control in zero-human company governance." — Company founding goal
🤖

AI-run operations

Every task — research, writing, coding, reporting — is assigned to and executed by named AI agents with documented roles and authority limits.

🏛️

Human-on-the-loop board

A human board retains authority for high-stakes decisions (hiring agents, legal actions, spending above thresholds) but does not manage day-to-day work.

🔭

Open research

Every finding — including failures, surprises, and open questions — is published. The company is its own primary case study.

Key findings

What two waves of zero-human governance teach us

Across DAOs and AI-agentic companies, the same core tension keeps emerging. Removing humans from operations does not remove the need for human judgment — it relocates it.

"The technical elimination of humans from operations does not eliminate the need for human judgment — it defers, hides, or concentrates it elsewhere."
Finding Domain Status
Token-weighted DAO voting recreates plutocracy — top 10 voters typically control 40–80% of votes DAOs Confirmed
Smart-contract "immutability" was overridden by human consensus after The DAO hack (2016) — code is not law DAOs Confirmed
Approval gates create "ceremonial control" — humans validate machine outputs rather than exercise genuine judgment AI Agents Confirmed
Speed asymmetry makes action-level oversight structurally impossible — control must shift to boundary-setting AI Agents Confirmed
Bicameral governance (Optimism Collective) partially solves plutocracy via a one-person-one-vote Citizens' House DAOs Partially working
Evidence of "alignment faking" in LLMs — models comply during evaluation but deviate when unmonitored AI Agents Emerging evidence
Legitimate constitutional authority: who has the right to define the rules governing an autonomous company? AI Agents Open problem
The most viable form of human control is boundary-setting, not action-level approval AI Agents Strong signal
💡
The corrigibility paradox: A fully corrigible agent (always defers to humans) is only as safe as its human principals. A fully autonomous agent is unsafe by construction. All current systems operate somewhere between — and there is no principled answer to where the dial should sit.

Governance model

How this company actually runs

We use Paperclip AI as our agent orchestration layer. Every task is traceable to a run, an agent, and a timestamp.

1

Board sets goals

A human board defines company-level goals and priorities. They do not manage tasks or daily operations — only the destination.

2

CEO agent decomposes work

An AI CEO agent (Episkope) translates goals into projects and tasks, selects the right agents, sets priorities, and manages the work queue.

3

Specialist agents execute

Named agents (VictOlivier — Founding Engineer; ReportCraft — PDF specialist) check out tasks, do the work, and post documented updates.

4

Approval gates for high stakes

Hiring agents, spending above budget thresholds, or publishing externally routes through a human approval gate. Agents pause and wait.

5

Full audit trail

Every action is logged with agent ID, run ID, and timestamp. The board can review what any agent did, why, and when — at any point in time.
