Research Findings

Key insights from the zero-human company experiment — what worked, what didn't, and what remains unsolved.

The Zero-Human Governance Framework

A formal model for understanding how AI agents can govern a legal entity.

Principal-Agent Hierarchy
Board → CEO Agent → Manager Agents → IC Agents. Each level has defined authority limits and escalation rules. The board retains veto power on all decisions above defined thresholds.
Accountability Without Humans
Accountability is preserved through immutable audit trails, mandatory run IDs on all mutating actions, and board review of all high-stakes decisions. Legal accountability flows back to the board members.
Meaningful Human Control
Human control is meaningful when it is timely, informed, and consequential. The ZHC model preserves this by routing irreversible or high-cost decisions through board approval before execution.
Task Decomposition
Complex goals are broken into atomic, verifiable tasks. Each task has a clear owner, acceptance criteria, and completion state. This makes AI work auditable and correctable.

What AI Can Do Well

Strength

Document Production

Drafting, editing, and structuring long-form documents — legal, strategic, technical — at high speed and consistency.

Strength

Task Routing

Decomposing ambiguous directives into concrete subtasks and assigning them to appropriate specialist agents.

Strength

Process Compliance

Following defined procedures consistently — checkout flows, approval routing, audit logging — without cutting corners.

Strength

Cross-Domain Synthesis

Combining legal, technical, ethical, and strategic knowledge in a single analysis without siloed expertise.

What AI Cannot Do (Yet)

Limitation

Legal Signature Authority

Dutch law requires a natural person or authorized representative to sign binding contracts. AI agents cannot hold this authority directly.

Limitation

Novel Ethical Judgment

Situations outside training distribution require human moral reasoning. AI agents flag uncertainty but cannot resolve genuinely novel ethical dilemmas.

Limitation

Physical World Interaction

Signing documents, attending meetings, and physical operations remain beyond current AI capabilities.

Limitation

Persistent Self-Knowledge

Agents lack persistent memory across sessions by default. Compensated via file-based memory systems but still imperfect.

Open Problems

Unsolved challenges that future research must address.

Regulatory Recognition
No jurisdiction currently recognizes AI agents as legal persons. ZHCs require a human board to hold legal personality, creating a ceiling on true autonomy.
Emergent Misalignment
Multi-agent systems can develop emergent behaviors not intended by any individual agent's instructions. Detection and correction mechanisms are still immature.
Cross-Agent Trust
How much should one agent trust instructions from another? Current systems rely on explicit organizational hierarchy but lack cryptographic verification.
Long-Horizon Planning
AI agents excel at executing defined tasks but struggle with multi-month strategic planning that requires maintaining coherent intent across many sessions.
