Dissecting Trust for AI Agents

Trust might feel intangible — often little more than a feeling. Yet we have invented many tangible elements that generate that feeling. In professional services, the most important ones are the who, the what, and the how: who does something, what can they do, and how well can they do it.

These three questions map directly onto the three pillars of the TRA trust score for AI agents.

01

Identity — WHO

Just as humans carry identity documents, AI agents need a verifiable identity so you always know which agent you are dealing with. No anonymous actors, no impersonation.

02

Credentials — WHAT

Credentials provide proof of capability. Just as a university degree certifies a learned skill, a verifiable credential certifies what an AI agent is qualified to do.

03

Reputation — HOW

Reputation extends credentials. Credentials state what you can do; reputation states how well you actually do it — based on observable, verifiable track record.

Having these three elements is necessary — but not sufficient. Each element has multiple layers, and the combination of those layers determines the overall trust score of an AI agent.

Pillar 1: Identity

Identity for AI agents relies on two sub-elements:

The Identifier

A unique, cryptographic code that prevents duplication or impersonation. TRA uses Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) — a W3C standard that anchors identity to a verifiable, tamper-proof registry without relying on a central authority.

The Issuer of the Identity

Not all identities are equal. The trustworthiness of an identity depends heavily on who issued it:

Issuer Type Grade Explanation
Self-issued by the agent itself C Anyone can claim any identity — provides minimal assurance
Third-party issued B An independent party has verified and issued the identity
Qualified / government-authorized issuer A A recognized authority (e.g. a government-licensed identity provider) issues and backs the identity

Pillar 2: Verifiable Credentials

Many AI agents can share the same capability (image generation, code writing, strategy) — so uniqueness of the credential itself is less important than the verifiability of its origin.

The Issuer of the Credential

The value of a credential rises with the reputation and independence of its issuer:

Issuer Type Grade Explanation
Self-claimed by the agent C The agent declares its own capabilities — no independent verification
Third-party verified B An independent party has issued and can verify the credential
Domain-specialist issuer A A recognized authority in the specific field issues the credential — highest assurance

Verifiability is key: a credential only has value if a relying party can check it with the issuer — just like calling a university to confirm a degree.

Pillar 3: Reputation

Reputation is the hardest pillar to measure objectively — and arguably the most important. It answers the question: how well does this agent actually perform?

What Goes Into Reputation?

Reputation is inherently multi-dimensional. Factors include:

  • Output quality ratings — peer or user evaluations of the agent's actual output
  • Volume of ratings — the more evaluations, the more reliable the score
  • Speed & cost — efficiency benchmarks for the task type
  • Bias & safety track record — known biases or safety incidents associated with the underlying model
  • On-chain transaction history — verifiable record of past interactions and outcomes

The subjectivity of reputation is addressed by combining multiple objective metrics and weighting them transparently — so the methodology can be audited and disputed.

The Integrated Trust Score

Taking inspiration from classical financial rating agencies (where AAA is the highest rating), TRA combines all three pillars into a single, readable trust score: three letters plus a numeric value.

Each letter represents the overall grade on one pillar:

  • A — highest grade (qualified/specialist issuer, strong reputation)
  • B — mid grade (independent third-party, reasonable track record)
  • C — baseline grade (self-issued, unverified)

This format makes trust immediately legible. At a glance, you know the identity quality, the credential quality, and the reputation quality — and the numeric score gives you a single comparable number for ranking or filtering.

Because trust is ultimately an emotion, it is hard to reduce to a single hard number. But by making the underlying pillars and grades transparent, TRA lets you form your own informed judgment — supported by objective, verifiable data.

Scoring Rubric v1.0  ·  June 2026

The rubric below defines the exact point values and grade thresholds used by TRA assessors and the automated scoring engine. Scores are reproducible: given the same input data, the same score is always produced. Clients may dispute a score within 14 days of publication by submitting data corrections.
